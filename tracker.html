Contingency rules, decided in advance

  • Interview weeks. A technical round converts that Wednesday to prep and slips the episode a week. Two such weeks are budgeted. A third drops the target to five episodes — that's the right trade, not a failure.
  • If the pill sorter converts to paid work. Paid client work outranks unpaid content. It displaces episodes 5 and 6, and December becomes four published episodes plus a delivered feasibility engagement.
  • October 21 checkpoint. Three episodes in: is an episode costing 6 hours or 10, and is anything landing? If it's 10, cut scope — three parts becomes two, ten minutes becomes six. Cut scope, never sessions.
  • No offer by mid-October. Reallocate one Wednesday per fortnight to applications and outreach. Episodes go monthly, December target becomes four. The job is priority one and the schedule should visibly bend to it.
  • November 25 is empty on purpose. Thanksgiving eve, and your recovery slot if you're a week behind. Don't spend it in advance.

Session shape

  • 5:00–5:15 — read last week's log, write down tonight's single deliverable
  • 5:15–6:45 — deep block one, the hardest task, no exceptions
  • 6:45–7:00 — break, away from the desk
  • 7:00–7:50 — deep block two
  • 7:50–8:00 — log it

Wednesday is thinking work: research, scripting, decisions. Saturday is mechanical work: recording, rendering, thumbnails, upload. Recording needs a quiet house and rendering is dead time; neither deserves your best three hours of the week.

Before you script any episode

Check NRND and end-of-life status against the manufacturer's own page for every part, note the datasheet revision on screen, and keep the manufacturer's spec visibly separate from what a hobbyist blog claims. That separation is the channel's whole credibility, and it's cheap if you do it from episode one.
